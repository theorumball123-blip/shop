How the printing works

If you're curious

A 3D printer builds things up in layers, each one about a tenth of a millimetre thick. A 3×3 slime is around two hundred layers. The 10×10 is closer to two hundred as well, but each layer is far bigger, which is why it takes a whole day.

The clever bit is printing the joints in place. Instead of printing parts and gluing them, the whole thing comes out as one object with tiny gaps left inside it — about a quarter of a millimetre — so the pieces are already loose when it comes off the bed. Get the gap wrong by a tenth of a millimetre and the whole thing welds solid.

That's also why they need working loose by hand. The first few movements break any last stringy bits between the parts, and after thirty or forty goes they run smoothly. I do that before anything goes out.

Looking after it

These are plastic

They're tougher than they look, but not indestructible. A few things worth knowing:

Don't leave them in a hot car or on a sunny windowsill — the plastic softens around 60 °C and things can sag out of shape. Wash with cold water and soap if they get grubby, not hot water. And the thin bits — dragon tails, the bike's handlebars — will snap if you bend them hard enough. If something breaks in normal use, tell me and I'll reprint it.

Who makes this

Sumner, Christchurch

Everything here is printed by me, at home, on one Ender 3 V3 SE. I started selling these at school and people kept asking for more, so I built this to keep track.

Most things come off the printer as a single piece with the joints already inside them, then I work every joint loose by hand before it goes out — that bit isn't automated, it's just me sitting there flexing them until they run smoothly. A few things, like the mini e-dirt bike, print in parts and get assembled instead.

Because they're printed one at a time in whatever colour you pick, no two come out quite the same. The rainbow and tri-colour ones especially — the colour shifts along the print, so yours won't look like anyone else's.

Questions

The ones I get asked most
How long does it take?

Small things are two to four days. The 10×10 slime is a full day of printing on its own, so allow about a week. Each product page says how long that one takes.

Can I pick the colour?

Yes — red, rainbow, tri-colour, or blue glow-in-the-dark. Rainbow and tri-colour shift along the length of the print, so every one is different.

Will it already move?

Yes. Most things print in one piece with the joints built in, and I work each one loose by hand before you get it. No glue, no assembly at your end.

What if it breaks?

Tell me. These are plastic and the thin parts can snap if you really go at them — if one fails in normal use I'll reprint it.

Can you print something I found online?

Often, yes. Send me the link and I'll tell you whether it'll work on my printer and what it'd cost. There's a box for the link in the order form.

Can you do a custom colour?

Only the four listed — those are the filaments I have. If you want something specific, ask and I'll tell you whether it's worth me buying a roll.

Do you do big orders?

Yes, but tell me early. Ten blanket fidgets is most of a day on the printer, so I need more notice than for one.

How do I pay?

When you get it, not before. Cash or bank transfer. Nothing goes through the website, so you're not typing card details anywhere.

Why is one side of the bike rougher?

It's printed in parts and assembled, and one side ends up tidier than the other. The second photo on its page shows the rougher side, so you know what you're getting.
